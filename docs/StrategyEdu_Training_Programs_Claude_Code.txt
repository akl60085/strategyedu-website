STRATEGYEDU TRAINING PROGRAMS PAGE — CLAUDE CODE SOURCE MATERIAL AND UPDATE INSTRUCTIONS

PURPOSE

Use this document as the content and positioning brief for revising the StrategyEdu training-programs.html page. Preserve the site's established visual design, navigation, responsive behavior, accessibility, and shared components. Refine the page copy and course structure to position StrategyEdu as a distinctive enterprise AI education provider grounded in deep experience with business intelligence, enterprise data, analytics, data architecture, and instructor-led training.

Do not present StrategyEdu as another generic provider of ChatGPT productivity classes. The catalog should tell a clear progression:

Business Intelligence -> Data Engineering -> AI Engineering

The intellectual backbone of the page is that successful enterprise AI depends on the same disciplines that have always supported trustworthy analytics: clean data, clear definitions, useful metadata, sound architecture, appropriate access controls, governance, and validation.


PRIMARY POSITIONING

Recommended headline:

From AI literacy to AI engineering.

Recommended supporting copy:

StrategyEdu helps organizations move from using generative AI safely and productively to connecting AI with the data, documents, and knowledge that power their business.

Retain the idea "A path, not a catalog." Present the offerings as a deliberate organizational learning journey rather than a disconnected list of classes.

The three tiers should be:

1. AI Foundations
2. Applied AI & Data
3. AI Engineering & Organizational Capability

The page should convey this progression:

AI literacy and responsible use -> practical business application -> enterprise data and AI integration -> sustainable internal capability


STRATEGIC STORY TO WEAVE INTO THE PAGE

Traditional business intelligence helped organizations answer:

What happened in the business?

Modern analytics increasingly helps answer:

Why did it happen?

Generative AI makes it possible to ask:

What does all of our information tell us, and what should I know about it?

However, AI can answer that last question reliably only when enterprise data and knowledge are clean, organized, governed, retrievable, and understandable. This is why the transition from data warehouses and BI dashboards to enterprise knowledge systems and AI applications is a natural evolution—not a completely separate discipline.

Emphasize that Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is fundamentally a data challenge as much as an AI challenge. StrategyEdu's differentiation comes from connecting established enterprise data practices with modern generative AI and AI engineering.


COURSE CATALOG

============================================================
TIER 1 — AI FOUNDATIONS
============================================================

Tier introduction:

Build a shared understanding of generative AI, learn practical prompting methods, establish responsible-use habits, and apply AI to everyday business work. These courses give employees a common language and a safe, productive starting point.


AI-101 — GENERATIVE AI FOR BUSINESS

Description:

Understand what generative AI and large language models are, how they fit into modern business workflows, and where human judgment remains essential. Participants explore the capabilities and limitations of leading AI assistants and learn to recognize strong, practical use cases in their own work.

Topic outline:

- What generative AI and large language models are
- How AI assistants generate responses
- Common business capabilities and use cases
- The roles of tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and Microsoft Copilot
- Strengths, limitations, and common misconceptions
- Hallucinations, uncertainty, and the need for verification
- Tasks suited to AI and tasks requiring human judgment
- Identifying useful starting points in everyday workflows


AI-102 — PROMPT ENGINEERING FOR BUSINESS

Description:

Learn a practical, repeatable approach to communicating with generative AI. Participants move beyond one-line questions and practice building prompts that provide the context, objectives, constraints, examples, and review criteria needed for dependable business results.

Topic outline:

- The elements of an effective business prompt
- Defining context, role, objective, audience, and desired output
- Adding constraints, source material, and success criteria
- Using examples to guide tone, structure, and quality
- Iterative prompting and constructive follow-up
- Working with documents and long-form source material
- Creating reusable prompt templates
- Asking AI to reveal assumptions and identify missing information
- Reviewing, validating, and refining AI-generated work

Use the phrase "Prompt Engineering" in the visible course title or prominent supporting copy. It is recognizable to corporate buyers while the description should keep the material approachable and business-focused.


AI-103 — RESPONSIBLE & SECURE AI USE

Description:

Use generative AI with appropriate care for confidential information, intellectual property, accuracy, fairness, and organizational policy. Participants learn how to recognize common risks, verify outputs, and keep people accountable for consequential decisions.

Topic outline:

- Confidential, proprietary, personal, and regulated information
- What should and should not be entered into public AI tools
- Hallucinations, factual errors, and misleading confidence
- Verification methods and source checking
- Copyright, intellectual property, and appropriate reuse
- Bias, fairness, and responsible decision support
- Human review and accountability
- Organizational AI policies and approved-tool boundaries
- Practical risk scenarios and safe-use guidelines


AI-104 — AI PRODUCTIVITY WORKSHOP

Description:

Apply generative AI to real business tasks in a guided, hands-on workshop. Participants practice improving work products and accelerating common workflows while maintaining accuracy, judgment, and accountability.

Topic outline:

- Drafting and improving email
- Summarizing and analyzing documents
- Research planning and synthesis
- Meeting preparation, agendas, and follow-up
- Reports, presentations, and executive summaries
- Brainstorming and structured problem solving
- Reformatting and adapting content for different audiences
- Developing reusable workflows and prompt patterns
- Reviewing outputs for accuracy, tone, and completeness

Position AI-104 as the practical culmination of Tier 1.


============================================================
TIER 2 — APPLIED AI & DATA
============================================================

Tier introduction:

Move beyond basic AI use to improve decisions, redesign workflows, analyze business data, work with proprietary organizational knowledge, and understand emerging automation capabilities. This tier should highlight StrategyEdu's AI-plus-data specialization.


AI-201 — AI FOR DATA ANALYTICS & BUSINESS INTELLIGENCE

Description:

Use generative AI to accelerate analytical work without sacrificing rigor. Participants learn how AI can help them understand datasets, develop and validate queries, explain business performance, identify patterns, and make analytics more conversational—while checking every conclusion against trusted source data.

Topic outline:

- Using AI to explore and analyze structured business data
- Explaining unfamiliar datasets, fields, and relationships
- SQL generation, explanation, debugging, and validation
- Developing and reviewing calculated metrics
- Supporting Excel, Power BI, Tableau, and other BI workflows
- Producing dashboard narratives and executive summaries
- Identifying trends, patterns, outliers, and anomalies
- Asking questions of data through conversational analytics
- Detecting hallucinations and unsupported analytical claims
- Validating AI-generated logic and conclusions against source data
- Protecting sensitive data in analytical workflows

Position AI-201 as a flagship StrategyEdu course.


AI-202 — AI FOR MANAGERS & BUSINESS LEADERS

Description:

Help managers lead practical, responsible AI adoption. Participants learn to identify valuable use cases, redesign work thoughtfully, set team expectations, evaluate AI-assisted output, and make informed decisions about investment, governance, and change.

Topic outline:

- Identifying high-value AI use cases
- Delegating appropriate tasks to AI
- Reviewing and approving AI-assisted work
- Establishing team norms and accountability
- Workflow analysis and redesign
- Assessing value, feasibility, risk, and return on investment
- Selecting processes suitable for automation
- Build-versus-buy considerations
- Governance, approved tools, and responsible adoption
- Employee engagement, training, and change management
- Moving from experimentation to repeatable business value


AI-203 — WORKING WITH YOUR COMPANY'S DATA

Description:

Learn what it takes for generative AI to work with an organization's own documents, data, and knowledge. This course bridges ordinary AI-assistant use and enterprise AI engineering by introducing the data preparation, retrieval, permissions, and governance concepts required for trustworthy answers grounded in company information.

Topic outline:

- General-purpose AI knowledge versus proprietary organizational knowledge
- Uploading files versus connecting enterprise knowledge sources
- Structured data versus unstructured documents
- Preparing documents and datasets for AI use
- Data quality, consistency, and source-of-truth considerations
- Metadata and why it improves discovery and retrieval
- Permissions, access controls, privacy, and governance
- Introduction to document chunking
- Plain-language introduction to embeddings
- Plain-language introduction to vector search
- Grounding AI responses in trusted corporate information
- Citations, traceability, and response validation
- Introduction to Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

Position AI-203 as the bridge between everyday generative AI training and the more technical AI engineering courses in Tier 3.


AI-204 — INTRODUCTION TO AI AUTOMATION & AGENTS

Description:

Explore how AI can move from answering questions to participating in structured business workflows. Participants learn the differences among assistants, automations, and agents; how AI systems use tools and business data; and where human approval should remain part of the process.

Topic outline:

- AI assistants versus workflows versus agents
- What tool use means in an AI system
- Structured inputs and outputs
- Connecting AI to documents, data, and business systems
- Breaking work into repeatable steps
- Human-in-the-loop review and approval
- Guardrails, permissions, monitoring, and failure handling
- Practical examples of automating repetitive knowledge work
- Evaluating feasibility, value, and risk
- Recognizing when conventional automation is the better choice

Keep this course at an executive/practitioner level. Do not describe it as a software-development class unless the page supports optional technical delivery formats.


============================================================
TIER 3 — AI ENGINEERING & ORGANIZATIONAL CAPABILITY
============================================================

Tier introduction:

Develop the technical understanding, data readiness, leadership alignment, and internal teaching capacity needed to move enterprise AI beyond isolated experiments. This tier connects trusted organizational information to AI applications and helps companies build durable capability.


AI-301 — BUILDING RAG APPLICATIONS WITH ENTERPRISE DATA

Description:

Learn how organizations connect generative AI to their own trusted information. Participants develop an understanding of the complete Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline—from source preparation and chunking through embeddings, vector search, retrieval, prompting, citations, and response evaluation.

Topic outline:

- RAG use cases and architecture
- Source identification and document ingestion
- Cleaning and normalizing source content
- Chunking strategies and tradeoffs
- Metadata design and filtering
- Embeddings and semantic representation
- Vector databases and search
- Retrieval, ranking, and context assembly
- Prompt construction with retrieved context
- Citation and source-attribution patterns
- Evaluating relevance, faithfulness, and answer quality
- Security, permissions, governance, and operational considerations
- Common failure modes and improvement strategies

Where useful, visualize the core pipeline as:

source data -> cleaning -> chunking -> metadata -> embeddings -> vector database -> retrieval -> prompt/context -> large language model -> cited response -> evaluation

Make the key message explicit: RAG is fundamentally a data problem as much as it is an AI problem.


AI-302 — PREPARING ENTERPRISE DATA FOR AI

Description:

Prepare the data and knowledge foundation that trustworthy AI applications require. Participants learn how familiar disciplines from data warehousing, analytics, architecture, and governance extend to enterprise AI systems that work across both structured data and unstructured content.

Topic outline:

- Data readiness for AI
- Data quality, completeness, freshness, and reliability
- Semantic consistency and shared business definitions
- Entities, dimensions, hierarchies, and relationships
- Metadata, lineage, ownership, and provenance
- Systems of record and source-of-truth conflicts
- Structured data versus unstructured information
- Document structure, cleanup, and preparation
- Taxonomies, classification, and retrieval-friendly organization
- Identity, permissions, and access controls
- Privacy, retention, security, and governance
- Evaluating whether enterprise information is AI-ready

Position AI-302 as one of StrategyEdu's most distinctive offerings. Draw a clear connection between preparing data for traditional analytics and preparing enterprise knowledge for AI:

data warehouse -> BI dashboard

enterprise knowledge -> AI application


AI-303 — AI CHAMPIONS / TRAIN-THE-TRAINER

Description:

Build a network of capable internal AI champions who can model responsible practices, support colleagues, facilitate learning, and sustain adoption after an initial training engagement. Participants strengthen both their AI fluency and their ability to teach and coach others.

- Reinforcing responsible and secure use
- Demonstrating practical, role-relevant workflows
- Teaching prompt methods clearly and consistently
- Facilitating hands-on exercises
- Coaching colleagues and answering common questions
- Recognizing issues that require escalation
- Creating reusable examples, job aids, and office hours
- Gathering feedback and identifying new use cases
- Measuring adoption, confidence, and business impact
- Building a sustainable internal learning community

1. Preserve a three-tier visual structure and make the progression immediately understandable.

   - Course code and title
   - Concise description

   - Tier placement

If the full topic lists make the page too long, use accessible accordions/details elements, modals already supported by the site, or a brief "Topics include" summary.    - AI-301 — Building RAG Applications with Enterprise Data

7. Avoid overstating delivery specifics that have not been supplied. Do not invent duration, price, prerequisites, certification, class size, delivery mode, or guaranteed outcomes. Preserve any verified existing facts already on the live page unless they conflict with this brief.

8. Do not add unsupported client names, metrics, résumé claims, testimonials, or technology partnerships. Use the founder's enterprise data and training background as positioning context only where the site already supports such biographical claims.

9. Preserve accessibility: semantic headings, keyboard-operable interactions, sufficient contrast, descriptive labels, and no important information available only on hover.
